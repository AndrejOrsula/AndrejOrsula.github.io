Space Robotics Bench

Simulation framework and benchmark for robot learning in space, built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim.

Space Robotics Bench (SRB) is an open-source collection of simulation environments and tasks for robotics research in space, from orbit to the surfaces of the Moon and Mars. It offers a single framework for developing and testing autonomous systems across these scenarios. Built on NVIDIA Isaac Sim, it runs many simulations in parallel and generates new variations of its scenes on demand, so policies train and are evaluated across many worlds instead of one.

Features

  • Parallel simulation: many environment instances run in parallel to accelerate learning and evaluation.
  • Procedural generation: terrains, rocks and other assets are generated on demand with SimForge, whose browser demo shows some of these assets in 3D.
  • Domain randomization: extensive randomization of the simulated scenes for robustness and generalization.
  • Gymnasium API: environments plug into standard reinforcement-learning frameworks.
  • ROS 2 interface: interoperates with the ROS 2 and Space ROS ecosystems.

Tasks cover manipulation, mobile robotics and mobile manipulation. Each task can run with different robots and in different space domains.

Status

The latest release is the 0.0.6 pre-release (October 2025); its documentation covers installation. It is dual-licensed under MIT or Apache 2.0. Its own assets and those generated with SimForge are released under CC0 1.0.

Publications

  • Space Robotics Bench: Robot Learning Beyond Earth, arXiv preprint, 2025.
  • Towards Benchmarking Robotic Manipulation in Space, CoRL 2024 Workshop on Mastering Robot Manipulation in a World of Abundant Data.
